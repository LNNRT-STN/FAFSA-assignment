\section*{Technical memo}

\subsection*{1. Accounting standards}
The liability is clear. \ac{ias}~32.23 says that a contract to buy own equity instruments for cash is a financial liability.
This rule also applies to a put on the shares of a subsidiary that a minority shareholder holds.
The liability is the present value of the redemption amount. The probability of exercise is not relevant.

The other side of the liability entry is not clear. \acs{ias}~32 does not say where to record the debit, or the later changes in value.
Two readings are possible.
Under \ac{ifrs}~10.23, a change in ownership without loss of control is an equity transaction, so changes in value go to equity.
Under \acs{ifrs}~9, the put is a financial liability, so changes in value go to \ac{pl}.

Practice varies.
The \ac{iasb} has tentatively decided that changes in value go to \acs{pl}, and that the initial debit is a deduction from minority interests.
There is no date for a final standard.\footnote{IASB Update, September 2026 (FICE), ifrs.org.}
Figure~\ref{fig:pnl} shows the effect on LVMH's net profit.

\subsection*{2. LVMH's accounting method}
LVMH applies a French method to puts given before 1 January 2010.\footnote{FD 2025, note 1.13, p.~33.}
The Diageo put is from 1994.
LVMH records the value of the put as a liability, and removes Diageo's minority interest from equity.
Without the put, equity includes Diageo's 34\% of \ac{mh} as ``minority interests''. With the put, this share is a liability, and LVMH must not show it twice.
LVMH records the difference between the liability and the minority interest in goodwill. All later changes in value also go to goodwill.

The entries show three facts.
First, the goodwill is a balancing figure, not an asset. It is not in the list of assets that LVMH tests for impairment every year.\footnote{FD 2025, note 5, p.~40.}

Second, the value change does not go to profit. In FY2024, LVMH recorded a credit of \euro4.4bn to goodwill. Under the \acs{iasb} decision, the same credit is a gain in \acs{pl}.

Third, Diageo is not in ``minority interests''. The \euro1,477m of minority interests in the balance sheet excludes Diageo's 34\% of \acs{mh}.\footnote{FD 2025, balance sheet, p.~26; note 18, p.~53.}

\subsection*{3. Valuation of \acs{mh}}
The Diageo liability is $0.80 \times 0.34 \times$ the fair value of \acs{mh}.
A change of \euro1bn in the value of \acs{mh} changes the liability by \euro0.27bn.
Because \acs{mh} is not a listed company, its fair value is a management estimate.

The method changed in 2025. Until mid-2025, LVMH applied peer multiples to \acs{mh}'s operating results.\footnote{FD 2024, note 21, p.~57; same text in FD 2021--2023.}
For FY2025, LVMH used a \ac{dcf} of \acs{mh}, with the method of its impairment tests.\footnote{FD 2025, note 21, p.~56.}
LVMH gives no reason for the change and no sensitivity.

The \acs{dcf} assumptions are generous. For \ac{ws}, LVMH uses a post-tax discount rate of 6.9\% and terminal growth of 2.1--3.5\%.
This is the lowest discount rate and the highest growth rate in the group.\footnote{FY2025 URD, note 5, p.~357.}

Diageo's numbers give a lower value. Diageo records its 34\% at \$5,107m. It says that a 10\% decrease in \acs{ebitda} causes an impairment of \$388m.\footnote{Diageo AR 2026, note 6, printed p.~157.}
Thus, $5{,}107 - 0.9\,V = 388$, and $V \approx \$5.2$bn for 34\%. At \$1 = \euro0.86, this gives approximately \euro13.3bn for 100\% of \acs{mh}, against LVMH's \euro17.4bn (Figure~\ref{fig:fv}).

\runin{Why the two values differ}Three causes explain the gap.
(1)~Method: LVMH's \acs{dcf} uses its own business plan and looks through the current decrease. Diageo uses an \acs{ebitda} multiple, which follows the market today.
(2)~Assumptions: LVMH's discount rate and growth rate are generous (see above).
(3)~Date and currency: 31 December 2025 in euro, against 30 June 2026 in dollars.
This effect is small: LVMH's own value changed by less than 1\% in H1~2026.

\runin{Why the difference matters}On Diageo's basis, the put liability is \euro3.6bn, \euro1.1bn less than booked.
But the same \acs{dcf} also supports the value of the \acs{ws} brands in the impairment test.
A lower value of \acs{mh} can cause an impairment of these brands.
The acquirer must not accept either value without a check.

\subsection*{4. Estimating the undisclosed liability}
LVMH does not disclose the Diageo liability separately.
Note~4 gives the goodwill part and note~18 gives Diageo's share of \acs{mh} equity. Their sum is the Diageo liability (Table~\ref{tab:series}).
The goodwill line also includes small pre-2010 puts (note~21 says ``mainly'' Diageo). Thus, the implied values are approximate.

LVMH recorded no material impairment loss for this decrease.\footnote{FD 2025, note 5, p.~40.}

\subsection*{5. LVMH's disclosures}
LVMH discloses the price formula, the notice period, the method (in one sentence), Diageo's share of profit, equity and dividends, and the exclusion from net debt.
It does not disclose the sensitivity of the value, the reason for the 2025 method change, the change-of-control terms, or Diageo's protections as a minority shareholder.

The put is not a key audit matter.\footnote{FY2025 URD, statutory auditors' report, p.~411.}
Diageo's report gives one more term: if a competitor takes control of Diageo, LVMH can make Diageo sell its \acs{mh} shares.\footnote{Diageo AR 2026, directors' report, printed p.~130.}
LVMH's report does not say what occurs if a buyer takes control of LVMH.

\subsection*{6. Implications for the acquirer}
\runin{Leverage}Add the put to net debt: \euro6.9bn becomes \euro13.2bn (Figure~\ref{fig:netdebt}).

\runin{Earnings}Under the \acs{iasb} decision, one line changes net profit by $-20\%$ to $+35\%$ in a year (Figure~\ref{fig:pnl}). Remove this line from covenants, earn-outs and bonus targets.

\runin{Price of 100\% of \acs{mh}}The Diageo put is \euro4.7bn, which is 80\% of a low value. At full fair value, it is \euro5.9bn; on Diageo's basis, \euro3.6bn. Diageo will not sell at a 20\% discount in a recovered market. The real cost is \euro4.7--12bn.

\runin{Cash and relationship}Diageo receives 34\% of \acs{mh} dividends (\euro141m in 2025).\footnote{FD 2025, note 18, p.~53.} In 2020, Diageo started arbitration about \euro181m of retained dividends.\footnote{Vinetur (Reuters), 4 August 2026.} The partner is a counterparty, not an ally.
