\section*{Technical memo}
LVMH reports under \ac{ifrs} as adopted in the EU. For the Diageo put, it applies \ac{ias}~32.23 and, for the debit side, the French ``partial goodwill'' method (Section~2).

\subsection*{1. Accounting standards}
Under \acs{ias}~32.23, an obligation to buy own equity instruments for cash is a financial liability, even if the holder decides whether to exercise.
Thus, the accounting standard is clear on how to book the credit side.
This rule also applies to a put on the shares of a subsidiary that a minority shareholder holds.
The liability is the present value of the redemption amount. The probability of exercise is not relevant in this case.

The other side of the liability entry is not clear. \acs{ias}~32 does not say where to record the debit, or the later changes in value.
Two readings are possible.
Under \acs{ifrs}~10.23, a change in ownership without loss of control is an equity transaction, so changes in value go to equity.
Under \acs{ifrs}~9, the put is a financial liability, so changes in value go to \ac{pl}.

Practice varies.
The \ac{iasb} has tentatively decided that changes in value go to \acs{pl}, and that the initial debit is a deduction from minority interests.
There is no date for a final standard.\footnote{IASB Update, September 2026 (FICE), ifrs.org.}
Figure~\ref{fig:pnl} shows the effect on LVMH's net profit.

\subsection*{2. LVMH's accounting method}
LVMH uses the ``partial goodwill'' method for puts given before 1 January 2010.\footnote{FD 2025, note 1.13, p.~33; AMF Recommendation 2009-28, \S3.4, p.~10 (``partial goodwill'' method).}
The Diageo put is from 1994.
LVMH records the value of the put as a liability, and removes Diageo's minority interest from equity.
Without the put, equity includes Diageo's 34\% of \ac{mh} as ``minority interests''. With the put, this share is a liability, and LVMH must not show it twice.
LVMH records the difference between the liability and the minority interest in goodwill. All later changes in value also go to goodwill.

Figure~\ref{fig:decomp} shows the two parts of the liability at end-2021 and end-2025.
Figure~\ref{fig:flow} shows one change in value under LVMH's method and under the \acs{iasb} decision.

\subsubsection*{Implications of the booking entries}
First, the goodwill is a balancing figure with no economic substance. Note~5 lists the assets that LVMH tests for impairment every year. This goodwill is not in that list.\footnote{FD 2025, note 5, p.~40.}

Second, the value change does not go to profit (Figure~\ref{fig:flow}).

Third, the balance sheet does not show Diageo in ``minority interests''. The \euro1,477m of minority interests excludes Diageo's 34\% of \acs{mh}.
However, the income statement still includes Diageo's share of profit (\euro177m in 2025) in minority interests.\footnote{FD 2025, balance sheet, p.~26; note 18, p.~53; note 1.13, p.~33.}

\subsection*{3. Valuation of \acs{mh}}

The Diageo liability is $0.80 \times 0.34 \times$ the fair value of \acs{mh}.
A change of \euro1bn in the value of \acs{mh} changes the liability by \euro0.27bn.
Because \acs{mh} is not a listed company, its fair value is a management estimate.

The method changed in 2025. Until mid-2025, LVMH applied peer multiples to \acs{mh}'s operating results.\footnote{FD 2024, note 21, p.~57; same text in FD 2021--2023.}
For FY2025, LVMH used a \ac{dcf} of \acs{mh}, with the method of its impairment tests.\footnote{FD 2025, note 21, p.~56.}

The \acs{dcf} assumptions are generous. For \ac{ws}, LVMH uses a post-tax discount rate of 6.9\% and terminal growth of 2.1--3.5\%.
This is the lowest discount rate and the highest growth rate in the group.\footnote{FY2025 URD, note 5, p.~357.}

Diageo's numbers give a lower value. Diageo records its 34\% at \$5,107m. It says that a 10\% decrease in \acs{ebitda} causes an impairment of \$388m.\footnote{Diageo AR 2026, note 6, printed p.~157.}
Assume that \acs{mh} has no material net debt. Then the value of the stake changes in proportion to \acs{ebitda}.
Thus, $5{,}107 - 0.9\,V = 388$, and $V \approx \$5.2$bn for 34\%.
Diageo's second sensitivity (a 1.0x lower multiple causes an impairment of \$312m) agrees: it gives a multiple of approximately 11.7x.\footnote{Diageo AR 2026, note 6, printed p.~157; own calculation.}
At \$1 = \euro0.88,\footnote{Closing rate that Diageo uses for the \acs{mh} balance sheet, AR 2026, note 6, printed p.~157.} this gives approximately \euro13.6bn for 100\% of \acs{mh}, against LVMH's \euro17.4bn at end-2025 (\euro17.5bn at 30 June 2026; Figure~\ref{fig:fv}).

\subsubsection*{Explaining the gap}
Two causes explain the gap.
The first cause is method: LVMH's \acs{dcf} uses its own business plan and looks through the current decrease. Diageo uses an \acs{ebitda} multiple, which follows the market today.
The second cause is that the dates and currencies differ (LVMH: euro, 31 December 2025; Diageo: dollars, 30 June 2026), but this effect is small: LVMH's own value changed by less than 1\% in H1~2026.

The same \acs{dcf} also supports the value of the \acs{ws} brands in the impairment test.
A lower value of \acs{mh} can cause an impairment of these brands.
The acquirer must not accept either value without a check.

\subsection*{4. Estimating the undisclosed liability}
LVMH does not disclose the Diageo liability separately.
Note~4 gives the goodwill part and note~18 gives Diageo's share of \acs{mh} equity (Figure~\ref{fig:decomp}).
The goodwill line also includes small pre-2010 puts (note~21 says ``mainly'' Diageo). Thus, the implied values are approximate.

Weaker results are one likely driver. \acs{ws} profit from recurring operations fell from \euro2,109m (2023) to \euro1,016m (2025).\footnote{FD 2025, management report 1.2, p.~9.}
Sector multiples fell until 2024. This fall and the 2025 change of method also affect the value.\footnote{Pernod Ricard EV/EBITDA: 16.5x (FY2023), 12.0x (FY2024), MarketScreener, retrieved 8 November 2026; sector de-rating since 2021: Recital, 26 March 2026.}

\acs{ws} intangible assets and goodwill decreased from \euro7,775m (2023) to \euro2,423m (2025).\footnote{FD 2025, note 24.1, pp.~61 and 63.}
Almost all of this decrease (\euro5.3bn of \euro5.4bn) is the goodwill on purchase commitments, which fell from \euro5,682m to \euro386m.\footnote{FD 2023 and FD 2025, note 4, p.~40.}
Thus, the decrease comes from the method, not from an impairment. LVMH recorded no material impairment loss.\footnote{FD 2025, note 5, p.~40.}

\subsection*{5. LVMH's disclosures}
LVMH discloses the price formula, the notice period, the method (in one sentence), Diageo's share of profit, equity and dividends, and the exclusion from net debt.\footnote{FD 2025, note 21, p.~56 (price, notice); note 1.13, p.~33 (method); note 18, p.~53 (profit, equity, dividends); note 19.1, p.~53 (net debt).}
It does not disclose the sensitivity of the value, the reason for the 2025 method change, the change-of-control terms, or Diageo's protections as a minority shareholder.

The put is not a key audit matter.\footnote{FY2025 URD, statutory auditors' report, p.~411.}
Diageo's report gives one more term: if a competitor takes control of Diageo, LVMH can make Diageo sell its \acs{mh} shares.\footnote{Diageo AR 2026, directors' report, printed p.~130.}
LVMH's report does not say what occurs if a buyer takes control of LVMH.

\subsection*{6. Implications for the acquirer}
The first effect is higher leverage. If the acquirer adds the Diageo put to net debt, net debt rises from \euro6.9bn to \euro11.6bn. With all purchase commitments, it rises to \euro13.2bn.\footnote{FD 2025, note 19.1, p.~53; balance sheet, p.~26.}

Earnings also become volatile, under the \acs{iasb} decision: one line changes net profit by $-20\%$ to $+35\%$ in a year (Figure~\ref{fig:pnl}). Covenants, earn-outs and bonus targets must therefore exclude this line.

Buying out Diageo may cost more than the booked put. Booked at \euro4.7bn, the put is 80\% of a low value. At full fair value, it rises to \euro5.9bn; on Diageo's basis, it falls to \euro3.7bn. The cost thus depends on the value of \acs{mh} and on the deal terms: from \euro4.7bn (the booked put) to about \euro12.5bn if \acs{mh} returns to its 2023 value.\footnote{Own calculation: \euro4,727m $\div$ 0.8; $0.8 \times 0.34 \times$ \euro13.6bn (Section~3); 2023: (\euro5,682m + \euro4,281m) $\div$ 0.8, FD 2023, notes 4 and 18, pp.~40 and 52.}

Note that cash also flows to Diageo. Each year, it receives 34\% of \acs{mh} dividends (\euro141m in 2025).\footnote{FD 2025, note 18, p.~53.} In 2020, \acs{mh} retained \euro181m of dividends, and Diageo started arbitration. \acs{mh} paid the dividend in 2021.\footnote{Vinetur (Reuters), 4 August 2026.} Hence, the interests of the two partners may not always align.
