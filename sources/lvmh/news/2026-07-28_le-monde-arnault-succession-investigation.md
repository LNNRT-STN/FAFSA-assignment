SUMMARY

- Le Monde published a six-part investigation (about six months of reporting) by Raphaëlle Bacqué and Vanessa Schneider into succession at LVMH.
- It describes rivalry and mistrust among Bernard Arnault's five children (Delphine, Antoine, Alexandre, Frédéric, Jean), all of whom hold senior roles in the group and are potential heirs; Arnault has spent years preparing the succession by giving them growing responsibilities.
- LVMH had raised the age limit for chairman and CEO to 85. Arnault (77) reportedly told his children in April to stop competing for his seat, and said at the AGM that succession would not arise for "seven or eight years".
- The investigation also covers strained relations between the children of his first marriage (Delphine, Antoine) and his second wife, Hélène Mercier.
- Arnault responded in a letter published by LVMH's press office, dismissing the report as tabloid-style and saying anyone "betting on a family rift" would wait a long time.
- Business circles are reportedly discussing an outside executive as successor, out of fear of a bitter conflict after his death.
